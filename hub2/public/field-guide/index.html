For facilitators

Running this with a group? You are the first trusted messenger the room meets — the way you hold the family is the method. Here's the short version; the full Facilitator & Training-of-Trainers manual goes deeper.

🧭 Five principles that hold the room
  • Never shame the messenger. Beth, Silas and Tom act from love — the arc is conversion, not blame.
  • Teach through people, not bullet points. Open on the scene before the framework.
  • Lead with a held value. Health, family, provision — arrive at rights from there.
  • Replace, don't just remove. Every retired line needs a new one to carry.
  • Make it safe to be uncertain. The tools are to think with, not to test people.
🌀 The session rhythm — every module runs the same five beats
  1. Arrive on the scene — read it aloud; \u201cwho recognises this?\u201d ~5 min
  2. Surface the idea — draw it out, then show 2–3 figures. ~10 min
  3. Run the tool — do it together, live. ~15 min
  4. Practise — apply it to their own case. ~15 min
  5. Commit — each names one change; capture it. ~5 min
📶 With devices & without

Every tool has a no-device version: draw the machine on a flip chart, hand out the four disinformation cards, fill the five reframing boxes on paper. Install the app once over wifi and it runs fully offline in the field; the printed job-aids carry QR codes back to the tools and audio.

🪜 Cascade it — training of trainers

Master trainers train facilitators, who run sessions and recruit champion aunties. The heart of a ToT is the micro-teach: each new facilitator runs one module for the group, and is certified when they can open on a scene, run the tool, and close on a commitment — without shaming anyone.

✅ Do & ✕ don't
Do
  • Open on a person and a scene.
  • Hand over a new line for the old one.
  • Close on a concrete commitment.
Don't
  • Open on a definition or statistic.
  • Leave a belief demolished, empty.
  • End on \u201cany questions?\u201d
